= Visualization Rendering Design
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

== Goals

Rendering preserves the meaning of the signal: RMS is a continuous energy
area, Peak is a discrete transient marker, and Spectrogram is chronological
frequency history. Palette choices and rendering reuse must not alter the
underlying audio data.

This document explains rendering choices and their tradeoffs. User controls,
defaults, and display behavior are documented in
link:01_config.adoc[Configuration and layout]. Cache ownership, invalidation,
and resource lifetime are covered by link:22_cache.adoc[Caching and Resource Lifecycles].

The implemented fixed-schema GPU materials, their recorded measurements, and the
proposed general rendering API are documented separately in
link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc[Parameter-Driven GPU Rendering Design]. The current
consumer behavior is described here; a proposal there does not change it.

[[analysis-boundary]]
== Analysis boundary

`rivu-visuals` receives immutable analysis frames, typed `VisualParameters`, and
localized `VisualLabels`, without depending on the application root, layout tree,
localizer or playback commands. The GUI presentation owner reconciles demand,
accepts source/seek identities and owns scheduling; the host routes Waveform seek
and load intents through existing playback and finite-render owners. Rendering
introduces no additional analysis worker, PCM pool or animation loop. Constructors
share configuration-projection defaults; history and GPU allocation are demand-driven.
Frequency views ingest shared analysis while Spectrum is visible or Spectrogram
is configured in the layout. Spectrogram history collection is independent of
presentation visibility; each style owns its rendering state.
Resource capacities and release points belong to
link:22_cache.adoc#visualization-caching[Visualization caching].

The audio worker writes one native-channel signal into a bounded shared-memory
window, before master volume. At 1× the tap follows the shared
link:06_visualizations.adoc#shared-visualization-settings[Before/After setting];
at other speeds it observes stretched output for either choice, with media time
advancing at playback speed. The GUI folds this stream into logical stereo for
`rivu-analysis`, outside both playback and paint callbacks. There is one FFT
path, not separate Before and After analyzers.
Each block carries its admitted output-frame position. GUI ingestion stops before
a block starting more than 1024 frames ahead of the observed audible cursor and
resumes on PCM or cursor notifications, without clock polling or deadline
prediction. This limits gross prefetch lead; it is not strict A/V synchronization.
Spectrum combines channel energy rather than averaging signed channels, so
opposite-phase stereo does not cancel. Optional spatial analysis retains the
channels' relationship.
Time-domain-only demand keeps the complete rolling window and RMS without
preparing FFT plans or frequency-domain storage. Frequency demand prepares those
resources lazily for the same window. RMS squares and accumulation use `f64`,
including the two-channel energy combination, before publishing `f32` values.

[[pcm-admission]]
=== PCM admission

The shared tap observes only PCM admitted to output. Its native-channel retention
covers bounded output prefetch, observer headroom and the maximum FFT span; exact
resource limits belong to link:22_cache.adoc#visualization-caching[Visualization caching].
Each refill admits at most 16,384 frames before returning to control handling.
DSP and monitor EQ process converted fragments once; later edits do not reprocess
pending output. Progressive waveform capture follows GUI-admitted PCM.

After and bypass taps borrow admitted PCM. A processed Before tap needs a
source-aligned FIFO bounded by graph latency plus one converted fragment
(at most two seconds of latency and 16,384 fragment frames, excluding preguard).
It belongs to the current capture identity: source/seek, signal, pause,
visualization enablement and capture restarts cannot relabel retained samples as current audio.
Optional-payload changes preserve admitted PCM; disabling capture releases the
FIFO on the next refill, even without output admission.

Continuity follows traversal-relative sample positions and explicit source gaps,
not comparisons of media timestamps. Seek/source replacement, confirmed gaps
and dropped analysis samples start new segments; ordinary clock corrections do
not. With a realtime graph, gap markers follow latency-trimmed output admission,
so an FFT cannot bridge a break or discard valid earlier pending audio.
A seek rejected before admission leaves decoder, output and analysis unchanged.
An admitted seek publishes a revision before backend execution. If the backend
rejects it, Core corrects the position with another revision: instantaneous GUI
analysis can be retired without a successful decoder seek. Obsolete rejections
cannot undo newer intent. Decoder seek execution failure stops playback rather
than resuming uncertain decoder state.

[[ingestion-and-publication]]
=== Ingestion and publication

Ingestion, analysis publication and presentation have separate triggers. One
validated stereo block feeds live analysis and Waveform, not separate Before/After
analyzers or per-panel readers:

        WF["Session Waveform summary"]

        R -->|"admitted samples"| W
        R -->|"same admitted samples"| WF
    end
    subgraph Publication["Analysis publication"]

        A --> F
    end
    subgraph Presentation["Window-local presentation"]
        V["VisualizerRuntime accepts current audio identity and demand"]
        H["Configured history collection, including hidden views"]
        G["Shared native-frame gate and cancellable display deadline"]
        T["Display time bounded by latest accepted audio timestamp"]
        P["Visible panels: staging, animation and GPU drawing"]
        F -->|"publication notification"| V
        V -->|"collect independently of native frames"| H

----

Shared-memory observation, identity fences and partial-tail admission follow
link:21_ipc.adoc#realtime-pcm-transport[Realtime PCM transport]; reader scratch is
bounded by link:22_cache.adoc#resource-boundaries[Resource boundaries]. Each
scheduling round processes at most 32 logical blocks and yields between
continuations. `analysis_rate_hz` limits fresh analysis results and demanded FFT
transforms, not PCM ingestion or invalidation publications. No fresh input means
no analysis publication timer.

Analysis always observes the latest complete window. When that window is shorter
than the analysis interval, a transient between observed windows can be absent
from Spectrum, Spectrogram, RMS and band-energy drive. This is a music-animation
sampling policy, not continuous metering; display interpolation cannot recover
unobserved audio. Density instead accumulates samples during ingestion.

Scope and stereo-density demand alone skip FFT transforms and musical-band
aggregation. They retain the same complete-window admission and publication
cadence, unwindowed channel RMS, and ingestion-driven density accumulation.
Frequency-domain consumers request their data explicitly; changing that demand
preserves the valid partial PCM window. Each publication carries its spectrum
request state alongside the optional axis and levels. An unrequested spectrum
does not invalidate retained drawing; a requested but empty spectrum is
unavailable. Consumers check signal identity and settings independently, so an
omitted payload cannot preserve drawing across a real semantic invalidation.

Spectrogram requests full FFT bins independently of the musical-band Spectrum
payload. With no musical-spectrum consumer, it skips that extra reduction and
its axis/level copies while retaining identical FFT analysis and history sampling.

Analysis preparation and retained analyzer capacity follow
link:22_cache.adoc#resource-boundaries[Resource boundaries]. Pause or loss of demand
clears capture input; disabling visualizations also breaks semantic continuity.
Backpressure never blocks playback. A stalled worker skips overwritten input
and marks a continuity break; PCM or cursor progress wakes recovery without polling. A
ready replacement and its generation publish together. Until then, invalidation
remains visible so consumers cannot smooth across unrelated audio.

=== Demand and continuity

Display enablement and optional payloads form one coalesced control state,
independent of the business-command queue. Closing a window relinquishes demand
even when that queue is full. Playback and visualization enablement remain separate gates;
display demand never starts playback. Capture restarts fence queued input and
late publications. Hiding configured history views does not restart capture.

Load, audition-source replacement and Seek reserve monotonic audio intent.
Settings admission compares the latest successfully queued output, signal and
visualization targets; unchanged settings reserve no new PCM boundary. Each queued
command carries that decision, and execution settles exactly its admitted fence,
including coalesced seeks. A display-only Apply therefore preserves the partial
analysis window while still validating the requested output; real target changes
and failed changed targets cannot relabel old PCM as current.
The visualizer runtime accepts only frames matching current display demand, the
actual audio epoch and applied intent. Current invalidation frames also reach the
runtime while replacement analysis is incomplete, retiring stale drawings without
waiting for the next FFT. Capture restarts and rejected seeks remain distinct from
a successful semantic source change.

Source/seek boundaries, Stop, dropped samples, FFT/window/signal or sample-rate
changes, and disabling visualizations break signal continuity. The producer owns its generation;
neither a delayed publication nor an optional-demand change implies an audio gap.
Changing only `analysis_rate_hz` adjusts scheduling without breaking continuity
or discarding the accepted sampling window and compatible presentations.
Views reject stale frames and reset envelopes or simulations rather than smoothing
across unrelated audio. Outside the retained pause/reveal handoff described below,
Spectrum withdraws its current bars and peak envelopes while analysis is invalid.
The next accepted window starts fresh envelopes; a retained drawing never seeds
those envelopes. Spectrogram starts a new pooling interval at a break; compatible
historical frequency buckets can remain visible. Its sample time follows accepted
audio, not wall time or the track's seek position; presentation interpolates only
within the accepted boundary described below.

Changing the selected signal resets the analysis sampling window and incompatible
history. Monitor EQ edits preserve realtime analysis history; new samples reflect
the edited processing. Neither operation automatically scans a track or clears
the rolling waveform. Active audition visualization uses the actual audible
source and admitted processing graph, not an arbitrary persisted graph recipe.

Instantaneous GUI analysis demand follows visible views; configured Spectrogram
and Waveform views retain collection demand while hidden. Stereo density, spatial
spectrum, integrated band energy, PCM, and full-bin Spectrogram data are independent
requests combined across panels; one accepted audio window and stereo FFT supply
them all. Optional demand changes preserve the sampling window, continuity
generation, and surviving consumers' display clocks. Losing all collection and
presentation demand fences capture.

Musical peaks, integrated band energy and Spectrogram reuse unnormalized bin power
when their demands overlap. Each retains its own reduction and calibration:
musical bands take maxima, energy bands sum power, and Spectrogram publishes dB
bins. Spatial analysis still reconstructs separate channel power for pan. Pool
exhaustion does not prevent current-window band-energy computation.

Demand is not payload readiness. An exhausted snapshot pool omits that optional
payload without blocking Spectrum, RMS, band power or other available payloads.
Density and Oscilloscope retain the last usable payload without pairing it with
newer metrics or timing. Spectrogram still advances its accepted time, so old
tiles can scroll; no column is inserted for the missing interval.

[[presentation-scheduling-and-visibility]]
=== Presentation scheduling and visibility

[[independent-analysis-and-display-clocks]]
==== Independent analysis and display clocks

The <<ingestion-and-publication,three scheduling rhythms>> share one window-local
`VisualizerRuntime` and staging across panels. Artwork and audio views use the
same native-frame gate and cancellable display deadline under the shared
link:01_config.adoc#animation[Animation FPS cap]. Positive limits share one time
origin, delivering due updates together; late deadlines coalesce without catch-up.
Scheduling a callback does not dirty the window: accepted input or animation
changes notify only affected panels. Configuration refreshes reuse unchanged
staging rather than treating it as new audio; neither animation clock refreshes
the business root.

Oscilloscope, Stereo Density and Spatial Spectrum wake for new analysis
publications and do not request animation frames between publications. This
presentation policy leaves analysis demand and history collection independent.

The display clock advances at wall-clock speed but never beyond the latest
accepted audio timestamp. Spectrum hold/release and smoothing, radial rotation
and smoothing, generative motion, and Spectrogram placement can therefore change
between analysis publications without another FFT or history insertion. Spectrum
also integrates the previous target up to the current display time before accepting
replacement levels. Release therefore advances even when every display tick consumes
new analysis, without applying the new target retroactively to the elapsed interval.
If the producer stalls, display time stops at the accepted boundary rather than
inventing audio. Generation/sample-rate changes and clock rewinds reset continuity;
the producer marks real sample gaps independently of publication cadence.

==== Pause, Stop, and semantic invalidation

Pause revokes live analysis and timed audio-driven animation, not the independent
finite Waveform request. The presentation owner holds the last live drawing by
withholding the worker's pause-reset publication, including on resume until fresh
input is ready. Resumption reanchors display time rather than replaying the
suspension. A paused business update can request one refresh without advancing
animation or triggering the idle fade; accepted finite data can update the preview.

Stop retires all analysis input. With `fade_when_idle` enabled, a confirmed physical
close of the displayed live source can fade its last bounded radial drawing for
two seconds, then release it; no worker audio is consumed while stopped. Core
publishes that source as `stopped_source_generation`, independently of statistics
persistence. An idle cached audio snapshot is not new close evidence. Repeated
Stop preserves the original cause and never restarts the fade or restores a
hidden, cleared or already released drawing. Clearing the queue, replacing the
source or an unmatched close clears the drawing rather than guessing continuity.

Semantic invalidations apply independently of producer continuity generation and
seek previews. Pause does not shield an old drawing from a source/seek or
FFT/window/signal change. Enablement has its own
<<visualization-enablement,acceptance fence>>.

==== Appearance changes without new audio

Radial color, geometry, sensitivity, and rotation-speed edits preserve the frozen
signal and animation time. A dB-range edit remaps the last accepted band peaks
without consuming another audio publication or advancing smoothing. Narrowing
then widening the range therefore recovers peaks clipped only for display.
Suspension and reveal reanchor radial animation time without recomputing its
integrated rotation phase from the current speed; speed edits preserve phase.

Presentation changes are not new audio: Spectrum and Spectrogram appearance,
range and axis settings invalidate only affected retained drawings and rebuild
from compatible working levels/history. Spectrum, Spectrogram and Waveform share
one signal palette; palette changes preserve Spectrum geometry and retained
Spectrogram scalar history.
Peak envelopes can evolve with markers disabled without invalidating visible
output solely for that animation step. Peak settings changes can still rebuild
the presentation.

Fractal color and bloom edits republish the frozen scene's material without
advancing its simulation or accepting audio. Hidden-window retirement still
prevents material edits from recreating released Fractal geometry.

Shared background changes affect both frequency views. A suspended panel whose
working data has been released cannot reconstruct that data from its final drawing.
It waits for fresh input after an incompatible mapping change; a final drawing cannot authorize
redrawing the prior source under a new source/seek or analysis identity.

Only affected visual panel entities are invalidated by analysis or waveform
updates. Cached scenes retain their measured panel dimensions until geometry or
visibility changes. Shared Spectrogram geometry changes invalidate sibling
scenes so every panel uses the current history dimensions. Scene caching is always
enabled. Playback, commands, and shutdown remain independent of presentation.

==== Visibility and layout retention

Hide cancels presentation clocks without treating visibility as a source change.
Demand is reconciled per panel; collection and drawing have separate permission
checks. Reveal reanchors display time and presents the latest retained history
without building GPU drawings while hidden. Final drawings, inactive-tab state
and GUI analysis-worker pools have distinct lifetimes defined in
link:22_cache.adoc#resource-boundaries[Resource boundaries]. Without reliable
visibility notifications, compositor throttling limits native animation callbacks
but not the worker's bounded analysis cadence. For visible outcomes, see the
link:06_visualizations.adoc#visualization-behavior[behavior matrix].

[[visualization-enablement]]
== Visualization enablement

Enablement gates GUI demand and the audio-worker tap. A transition generation
rejects queued samples and late finite results even across a quick off/on switch.
Live analysis needs a new complete audio window on resumption; Waveform's retained
data does not backfill missed PCM. Retention and release are described in
link:22_cache.adoc#visualization-caching[Visualization caching].

Disabling cancels the GUI's finite request; cancellation is not a load failure
and does not free the physical worker before it exits. This gate belongs to the
GUI, not the finite-render endpoint. For controls and visible outcomes, see
link:06_visualizations.adoc#visualization-enablement[Visualization enablement].

[[waveform]]
== Waveform

Core and audio execution own PCM capture/rendering, not waveform statistics.
The GUI keeps separate realtime and finite accumulators. Panels share the GUI's
selected display frame, not a summary that both producers write:

[source,mermaid]
----
flowchart TB
    RT["Realtime reader: fold admitted PCM with fenced source and traversal"]
    S["Transport-owned rolling summary"]
    Q["Explicit request: target, Before/After, and start revision"]
    F["Finite worker: fold PCM into a separate bounded summary"]
    M["GUI: validate request and source, merge into full preview"]
    P["Shared display: matching full preview, otherwise progressive frame"]
    RT --> S
    Q --> F
    F -->|"partial or complete scan frame"| M
    S -->|"same-source visits after request start take precedence"| M
    M -->|"full preview"| P
    S -->|"progressive frame"| P
----

Progressive collection is the default. Only admitted PCM fills bins; undecoded,
skipped or overwritten intervals remain blank, distinct from known silence.
Collection therefore follows the GUI subscription, not decoder activity alone.
Unknown or underestimated timelines coarsen existing bins to preserve the bound.
Subsequent playback can replace visited bins in the GUI preview, including
reducing old Peak and RMS values after lower-gain processing.
Contiguous fragments accumulate without counting an accepted prefix twice.
The first admitted PCM block supplies the realtime summary's sample rate;
catalog metadata does not calibrate its bins. PCM media start and step locate
samples relative to the selected source/CUE. Offset reads advance that start
by the accepted frame offset times the step.
Epochs, gaps and applied intent fence traversal and mapping boundaries rather
than deriving positions from Core progress notifications.

An explicit full load targets the current track if present, even when Stopped;
otherwise it uses the Track details target. A current track binds the scan to
the playback source generation only when not Stopped. The previous display
remains through request admission until the first valid finite PCM arrives.
The finite endpoint honors the requested Before/After independently of the
realtime speed rule. Core captures the actual source and processing state when
servicing a bound request, including retained PCM for memory-backed audition.
An unbound request opens the original source using its saved decoder hint; it
does not apply a persisted graph. After is a rerender, not past audible output.

The request's start revision protects newer realtime visits from a late scan
baseline. Merge updates the GUI full preview, never the transport summary.
A failed scan reports the error and can leave accepted partial data visible,
but cannot mark it complete. Finite blocks do not require whole-track PCM
retention. See link:21_ipc.adoc#finite-pcm-rendering[Finite PCM rendering]
for protocol and cancellation lifetime.

An open playback source retains its decoder until reload, independently of the
saved hint used by a new explicit preview. If there is no current track, a
configured Waveform in a visible window keeps the fallback Track query current
while visualizations are enabled, even with Details closed. That demand requests
neither artwork nor an editor.

Display aggregation keeps left and right statistics separate. Peak takes the
maximum known value; RMS uses decoded-sample-weighted energy,
`sqrt(sum(rms_i^2 * count_i) / sum(count_i))`, before display gain and gamma.
Channel fallback uses matching mono values and mono counts. Missing intervals are
not zero-valued samples. Bounded summary storage trades whole-track time
resolution for memory; it does not introduce zoom or interpolation. See
link:22_cache.adoc#waveform[Waveform caching] for the summary and GPU column bounds.

RMS fills extend above and below the centerline for the respective stereo
channels. Each display column has its own gradient endpoint, preserving color
differences between adjacent columns with different energy. The gradient starts
at a dimmed, desaturated palette minimum on the centerline and brightens toward
that column's RMS edge. Color compression uses an exponent of 1.2 for RMS
and 1.35 for Peak, preserving access to the full visible palette range.
These are visual choices, not amplitude or renderer limits.
Peak is a thin, solid marker at the channel's peak level. Color compression
does not change the height represented by the amplitude mapping.

Each panel supplies numerical stereo RMS and Peak columns: unknown components
draw nothing, while known silence retains a minimum-height fill. Each RMS fill
blends its own floating-point center/endpoint colors rather than using one shared
vertical gradient; Peak uses its endpoint directly. Logical column boundaries and
ceil-height geometry preserve one-logical-pixel markers through resize and scaling.
Cursor and glow remain dynamic. The closed GPU interface is defined in
link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc#internal-led-vertical-slice[Publication and drawing API].

[[spectrum]]
== Spectrum

For non-Solid styles, bar layout is either automatic width or a fixed count;
the configuration carries only that mode's parameter. Automatic layout derives
the count from plot width, requested bar width and gap. Solid uses the drawable
logical-pixel width. All modes remain bounded by the renderer's slot limit and
available width; layout does not change FFT resolution.

Spectrum Bars and LED submit compact GPU rectangle instances: one rectangle per
visible display slot. LED uses a bottom-aligned 3-pixel segment and 2-pixel gap
pattern in logical coordinates, including the partial segment at the body's top,
rather than tessellating every segment on the CPU. Outline uses up to four
nonoverlapping edge rectangles per visible slot. Line and Solid retain their
curve and fill geometry through the shared GPU Path material.
Solid fill closes at the plot baseline. Display slots at or below the configured
decibel floor remain empty rather than creating a minimum-height colored band.

All styles normalize the gradient to the full plot bounds, with the strongest
color at the top and the visible minimum at the bottom. A curve below the plot's
ceiling neither stretches to fill it nor acquires a local-height gradient.
Spectrum, Spectrogram and Waveform share a signal palette. Deadbeef starts at
deep blue rather than black; scientific color maps retain their complete standard
ranges. Palette selection does not change intensity, RMS mixing, Peak mapping or
signal geometry. See link:06_visualizations.adoc#visualizations[palette options].

Peak markers are discrete, solid-colored markers drawn separately from the body.
They are not connected into a curve, preserving their transient-marker meaning.

Rectangle coverage uses shared MSAA to avoid darkening adjacent zero-gap columns.
Geometry reuse and capacity belong to link:22_cache.adoc#spectrum[Spectrum caching];
GPU schemas and admission belong to
link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc#implemented-fixed-schema-slices[GPU rendering].

== Audio Visualizer ownership

All eleven styles use one `audio_visualizer` panel kind. Layout instances retain
style, reference lines, and labels, while style-specific global parameters keep
their existing configuration groups. Titles resolve from each instance's style.

Analysis and simulation results are shared by style, not panel: multiple panels
do not duplicate a field or advance simulation independently, and distinct styles
do not overwrite each other's state. Overlay toggles are paint-only. Retention
across tab changes, hiding and destruction is defined in
link:22_cache.adoc#resource-boundaries[Resource boundaries].

=== Radial styles

The radial presentation follows Noctalia Shell v5's concept and parameter model;
see the pinned
https://github.com/noctalia-dev/noctalia/blob/f95e95cafde8c23f1d3a62b969e2b5717c96d741/src/ui/visuals/fancy_audio_visualizer.h[`FancyAudioVisualizer` interface (v5.0.1)].
It does not reuse Noctalia's rendering implementation or promise its pixel output.
GPUI-native paths display at most 32 logarithmic bands. Accepted audio updates
shared targets; native ticks smooth and rotate them on the bounded display
clock. Repainting, resizing or replaying unchanged audio does not advance state.
Mapping changes can initialize from compatible retained input without inventing
audio time. Idle fade is a separate wall-clock overlay; radial styles have no
reusable rendering cache.

Bar glow uses two fixed widening translucent passes. Bloom intensity scales
their opacity; zero skips both passes. Pass count is renderer policy, while
rotation, geometry, opacity and intensity remain presentation settings.

=== Oscilloscope

Oscilloscope consumes the shared <<analysis-boundary,realtime tap>> as
unwindowed stereo PCM rather than reconstructing samples from FFT bins.
Only visible Oscilloscope instances request PCM.
The worker shares immutable snapshots of the latest bounded PCM window. Pool
exhaustion omits optional PCM without blocking audio or other analysis. Panels
share the selected sweep, never old PCM paired with newer timing or metrics.
Snapshot-pool capacities and release points belong to
link:22_cache.adoc#resource-boundaries[Resource boundaries]; configuration ranges,
defaults and panel controls belong to
link:06_visualizations.adoc#audio-visualizer[Audio Visualizer].

A rising zero crossing on the left anchors the sweep, with a right-channel trigger
for right-only audio. To leave room for trigger search, the sweep normally uses
at most half the exported PCM snapshot, not half the FFT window. Without a
crossing, it uses the latest complete sweep. Trigger selection happens once per
accepted publication.
Painting partitions the sweep into at most one bucket per physical pixel,
preserving finite minimum and maximum amplitudes in each channel instead of
dropping peaks. Invalid amplitudes are omitted. Gain scales drawing before
clipping, without modifying retained PCM or playback volume. Channel overlay
changes drawing only, not PCM or trigger selection. The represented duration is
the actual source endpoint duration `(samples - 1) / sample_rate`, rather than a
promised span longer than the available buffer.

Hiding the style releases the GUI's retained PCM, keeping only a bounded compact
normalized final sweep under the resource limits linked above. Disabling
visualizations discards that drawing too. Prepared analyzer storage can remain
reusable across either transition; actual PCM owners and input identities are
retired, so restoring cannot replay pre-hide PCM. Pause freezes the latest
displayed sweep without another refresh timer.
Resume or reveal replaces it when a current complete PCM window arrives; Stop,
source/seek invalidation, incompatible analysis settings, and disabling visualizations clear it.
With no valid sweep, only the background and enabled guides/labels are shown;
there is no waiting-for-audio message.
This style has no FFT windowing, automatic gain, dB color mapping, trails, or idle
fade, and it does not change the independent track-preview Waveform panel.

=== Stereo analysis styles

Stereo Density uses a fixed 256 by 256 mid/side field with decay normalized to
accepted audio time. Silence advances decay without creating a false center
point. Correlation requires energy in both channels; balance and side ratio
require nonzero total energy. Undefined metrics remain absent, not fabricated
as zero. Spatial Spectrum maps individual FFT bins by frequency and channel
power balance into a fixed 256 by 128 field. Cells retain the strongest power
rather than summing crowded logarithmic rows, which would make color depend on
bin density instead of signal level.
Spatial frequency mapping follows the accepted frame's sample rate and bin count,
not settings awaiting a new frame. Calibrated linear bin power preserves
single-sided normalization including Nyquist without a CPU dB-to-power round trip;
the field excludes DC.
Overflowing power is omitted rather than promoted to a saturated bright cell.

Both styles share one fixed-resolution numerical GPU publication per style across
panels. Enlargement neither increases source resolution nor advances analysis.
Semantic continuity breaks revoke the publication; hiding retains only that final
field and releases CPU field/history/mapping storage. Active color/range changes
republish from accepted fields, without another FFT or a CPU-generated image.
Changes made while hidden remap the retained numerical publication on reveal,
even while paused; this neither replays audio nor advances decay. The displayed
dB range follows the publication, including while a replacement is pending.
There is no application image-retirement queue.

Color ramps derive from the configured visualizer colors, with increasing
lightness and pale highlights reserved for the strongest cells. Density has a
cyanward intermediate hue; Spatial Spectrum retains the primary-to-secondary
hue progression. Opacity lifts faint cells without renormalizing their fixed dB
references; empty cells remain transparent. Palette quantization, color-first
interpolation and the sampling/upload cost boundary are defined in
link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc#different-consumers-and-interface-extraction[Field sampling].

[[spectrogram]]
== Spectrogram

Spectrogram maps calibrated non-DC FFT bins to colored columns, independently of
Spectrum's musical display bands. Opposite-phase stereo channels retain their
power. Stable history is a natural reuse boundary: newly accepted audio changes
recent columns without requiring the entire visible history to be rebuilt.
Frequency-row reduction and frames sharing a time bucket use maximum pooling
rather than averaging, retaining narrow peaks and short transients. Display
mapping can recolor compatible retained data without re-running audio analysis.
A gap starts a new interval instead of drawing new audio across missing time.
Seek and track replacement append subsequent accepted audio without erasing the
window. Source-rate changes retain a common physical-frequency domain so older
bands remain meaningful; new sources leave unsupported frequency bands blank.
Explicit FFT/window/signal setting changes still invalidate incompatible history.

Only accepted analysis frames write or pool history. Native display ticks move
the time cursor and tile placements without rebuilding unchanged numerical
publications; portions newer than the display cursor remain clipped.

A new peak, interval extension or ring overwrite dirties the containing tile.
The next presentation examines at most 32 columns per dirty tile. Changes to
history geometry or sampling invalidate the full tile cache. Scrolling only
adjusts tile placements. Frequency labels reuse their formatted values while
their physical domain is unchanged. Reveal refreshes a changed hidden domain
even without another live audio frame.

The horizontal scale is fixed by the configured history duration. New audio
enters at the right and scrolls left with accepted audio time; startup history
occupies only its actual duration, leaving the rest blank. No expansion or
release stage stretches retained columns. Paused or stalled display time freezes
placement, and resizing reprojects the same chronological intervals.

Frequency reduction preserves peaks; interpolation smooths known samples without
lowering them. Resizing reprojects chronological intervals but cannot recover
details already pooled at coarser resolution. Shared history capacity, sampling
resolution and resizing policy are defined in
link:22_cache.adoc#spectrogram[Spectrogram caching].

== Generative light

Fractal Beams uses fixed recursive topology rather than rebuilding random
branches on each frame. Three integrated frequency bands drive different
spatial scales; root-to-tip pulses follow rises in energy, not beat estimates.

Fractal Flow uses three spatial scales of a deterministic curl field. Bands
deform the field rather than steering independent random particles. Trails fade
and taper; boundary respawns discard old points rather than connecting opposite
edges. The fixed particle history is allocated on the first valid audio frame.

Both styles take band power from the stereo FFT bins with window-energy normalization,
not from summed dB values or maximum-pooled display bands. Opposite-phase
channels retain their energy.

A first valid frame seeds geometry; display ticks advance simulation toward the
latest accepted band-power targets. Each energy rise injects one onset, not one
per tick. Paint and resize never advance the scene. After a pause or inactive tab,
fresh input reanchors time without catch-up or a synthetic onset. Inactive styles
do not request band-power analysis. Real sample gaps reset simulation; sparse
publications alone do not. Scene and final-drawing retention follow
link:22_cache.adoc#resource-boundaries[Resource boundaries].

Glow uses widening translucent strokes, not image feedback or blur. Three ordered
layers share segment endpoints. Once all strokes fall below visible strength,
the empty drawing is retained without repeated GPU publication or repaint;
simulation continues so renewed audio can reveal the evolving scene.
Fixed topology, particle history and pulse slots bound storage; Flow integrates
with bounded substeps and rebuilds segments only from the final state.

FFT/window or signal changes revoke scenes until compatible audio arrives.
Color and Bloom republish material without advancing geometry; background only
repaints. Sensitivity and display range affect future accepted audio, preserving
paused geometry.

[[artwork-composition-and-animation]]
== Artwork composition and animation

Artwork styles change composition, not acquisition or signal analysis. Card
rotates the full square cover, rounded frame, and shadow together, reserving
diagonal margin rather than cropping the source composition. Metal's sheen and
Duotone's blue/purple glow remain fixed over or behind the rotating circular
cover; neither requires extracted colors or audio data. Real and generated
default covers share animation, interaction, and lifecycle rules. Style and
frame-pacing changes preserve the rotation angle. Style controls, per-panel
persistence, defaults, and the frame-rate range are documented in
link:01_config.adoc#artwork[Artwork].

The default cover is separate from the application icon, retaining decorative
rings and a full square composition. GUI styles apply the same corner treatment
to real and default covers; MPRIS leaves clipping and corners to the media host.

Artwork shares the window frame gate with audio views but has separate rotation
and source-image clocks. Rotation integrates only visible playback time;
suspension clears the tick anchor so reveal does not catch up. Source-animation
demand separately requires window activation; focus loss alone does not stop
rotation. Reduced motion cancels scheduled artwork animation without coupling it
to audio visualization enablement.

Each element has its own source cursor over shared decoded frames. The request
cap can skip source frames without slowing their timeline. Catch-up is bounded
by frame count; all-zero-delay sources advance one frame per redraw. A frame
invalidates only visible rotating instances or eligible animated-source consumers.
Source or style changes can invalidate all affected consumers. See the
link:06_visualizations.adoc#visualization-behavior[behavior matrix] for user-visible states.

== Failure boundaries

Views without accepted data do not invent signal history. Analysis frames with
incompatible FFT settings are ignored; Spectrum and Spectrogram reject mismatched
frequency and level counts. Explicit full-waveform decoding failures preserve
an error result rather than installing an incomplete scan as complete.
Before a full scan starts, its Track query must still match the selected track's
metadata stamp and request generation; unrelated library edits do not invalidate
that query. Once decoding starts, ordinary metadata changes do not cancel it:
the source, saved-hint, request-generation, and suspension checks described under
<<waveform,Waveform>> govern pending and completed previews.

Panels can report application-side errors such as Spectrum tessellation,
GPU publication construction or scene recording through `paint_gpu`. Accepted
analysis clears data-consistency errors; successful application-side paint can
clear rendering errors even while paused. That success does not acknowledge GPU
submission or presentation. Backend material rejection and shared-scene failure
are described in link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc#admission-and-retirement[Admission and retirement].
Stereo-analysis publication/paint failures revoke stale GPU content while retaining
accepted CPU values. Pending publication gets at most one attempt per ordinary
update, including unchanged input, until success; no retry timer or catch-up loop
is added. An invalid mapping is rejected before publication and waits for valid
configuration. Success also clears its error-reporting latch.
These rendering failures do not issue playback commands or make the audio
callback wait for graphics. Very small plots can omit geometry rather than
attempting invalid dimensions.

== Evaluating tradeoffs

The design moves work between aggregation, geometry construction, image
generation, retained memory, and scene submission. A cache hit or a lower
submission count alone is not evidence of an overall speedup.

Compare equivalent data, panel dimensions, display settings, and repaint rates.
Include first draws, stable repaints, changing data, and dense Spectrum styles;
measure the actual renderer as well as CPU and memory use. Renderer-level
changes should be justified by those workloads rather than historical CPU
percentages from a different implementation.

[[pinned-gpu-extensions]]
== Pinned GPU extensions

Rivu needs extensions to GPUI's image transforms and shared GPU materials rather
than a framework replacement. The pin, platform and maintenance boundaries, and
regression commands are authoritative in
link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc[Pinned renderer maintenance and GPU rendering].

=== Why the patch is needed

Artwork reuses its decoded image and atlas texture while the GPU transforms
the already-cropped sprite. Rounded clipping follows image-local coordinates;
the surrounding content mask remains in screen coordinates. No rotated pixel
buffers or angle-indexed image cache are created. Image transforms pass through
the existing sprite rendering path, with antialiasing coverage kept in screen
pixels under scaling and rotation.

Spectrum fills its geometry with the shared GPU palette material, retaining the
full standard color maps or authored Deadbeef cubic rather than approximating
them with two stops; see <<spectrum,Spectrum>>.

The extensions supply the fixed schemas and consumer/resource contracts described
in link:25_gpu_rendering.adoc#implemented-fixed-schema-slices[Implemented fixed-schema slices].
Radial Bars, Rings and Bars + Rings retain ordinary GPUI paths; the general
`Program` and generated-schema interfaces remain proposals.
